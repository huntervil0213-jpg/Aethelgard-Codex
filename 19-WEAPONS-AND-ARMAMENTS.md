# 19 — WEAPONS & ARMAMENTS

## Purpose

Aethelgard's weapons are tied to culture, material science, magic, military history, and individual fighting styles. Names should mean something rather than being decorative labels.

## Weapon Naming System

Every major named weapon can be recorded with:

- Name
- Literal meaning
- Cultural origin
- Weapon category
- Material
- Magical properties
- Intended wielder
- Fighting style
- Age
- Known owners
- Historical events
- Current location
- Reputation
- Known weaknesses

## Ancient Weapon Categories

### Swords
Straight or curved blades used for cutting and thrusting. Ancient swords may carry bloodline enchantments, elemental bindings, or rune arrays.

### Greatswords
Large two-handed blades designed around reach and force rather than speed.

### Rapiers
Precision weapons associated with fencing, dueling, court defense, and agile combat.

### Axes
Dwarven and northern cultures developed numerous axe traditions, ranging from mining tools converted into weapons to purpose-built battle axes.

### Hammers
War-hammers combine blunt force with armor-breaking design. Kaelen's hammer is particularly suited to his strength and close-quarters fighting.

### Spears
Among the oldest weapons in Aethelgard. Elven bone-spears demonstrate how a simple weapon can become technologically advanced through magical materials.

### Bows
Used by hunters, scouts, soldiers, and magical archers. Different cultures developed distinct bow geometries.

### Crossbows
Favored by soldiers who value repeatable mechanical performance over magical training.

### Daggers
Tools, backup weapons, ritual objects, assassins' weapons, and utility blades.

### Whips
Flexible weapons capable of entangling, disarming, or striking around defenses. The Skull-Veil spine-whip is a particularly feared variant.

### Staves
Walking staffs, ritual focuses, arcane conduits, and battlefield weapons can all originate from the same basic form.

## Materials

### Common Steel
Reliable and widespread.

### Dwarven Forged Iron
Dense, durable, and highly valued for mechanical weapons.

### Dragonbone
Extremely difficult to acquire and historically associated with draconic cultures.

### Elven Bone
Certain magically preserved bones can retain energy conduction properties for generations.

### Cyan Ley-Glass
A highly conductive magical material used in advanced weapons, barriers, and machinery.

### Obsidian Ash
Rare volcanic material capable of interacting unpredictably with certain forms of magic.

### Star-Iron
A legendary material associated with objects that fell from the heavens during ancient celestial events.

## Weapon History Through the Ages

### Before Kingdoms
Weapons were primarily hunting tools, ceremonial objects, and survival implements.

### Early Civilizations
Metallurgy and magical crafting transformed weapons into symbols of authority.

### Age of Dragons
Dragonbone, scale, crystal, and elemental forging became major traditions.

### Age of Concord
Weapon techniques spread between races through trade and shared military alliances.

### Age of Extraction
The development of Ley-Tech introduced weapons that could store and discharge magical current.

### Spire Era
Imperial militaries increasingly combined conventional arms with artificial magical focuses.

### Fracturing Age
Unstable magical conditions made certain high-powered weapons dangerous to their own wielders.

## Design Rule

A named weapon should feel like it could have existed before the current scene. Its history, construction, and reputation should reinforce the character who carries it instead of functioning as unexplained equipment.
