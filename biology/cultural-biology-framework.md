# Aethelgard Species Biology & Cultural Framework

This reference records non-graphic biological and cultural information for intelligent peoples and creatures in Aethelgard. It is designed to support continuity, character design, medicine, ancestry, reproduction, kinship, and worldbuilding without turning anatomy into explicit sexual content.

## Standard profile for every sapient species

Each species entry should eventually track:

- Origin and ancestry
- Typical lifespan
- Physical development stages
- Height and build ranges
- Skin, scales, fur, feathers, hair, or other integument
- Skull and facial structure
- Eyes and vision
- Ears and hearing
- Nose or equivalent sensory structures
- Mouth, teeth, jaw, and tongue
- Neck and throat
- Shoulders and torso
- Arms and forelimbs
- Hands, claws, talons, or equivalent manipulators
- Digits and joints
- Hips and legs
- Feet, hooves, paws, or equivalent locomotion structures
- Tail or other balance structures
- Wings or flight structures where applicable
- Internal organs and major physiological differences
- Blood or equivalent circulatory fluid
- Respiratory system
- Digestive system
- Nervous system
- Magical organs, conduits, glands, or resonance structures where applicable
- Reproductive biology at a non-graphic anatomical level
- Fertility and gestation/incubation patterns
- Birth or emergence process
- Parenting and kinship customs
- Sexual orientation and relationship norms where culturally relevant
- Gender concepts where culturally relevant
- Body-language conventions
- Similarities to other Aethelgard peoples
- Distinguishing anatomical traits
- Common misconceptions between races
- Medical vulnerabilities
- Environmental adaptations

## Reproductive framework

Reproduction is recorded clinically and non-graphically. Entries should establish whether a species is sexually reproducing, asexual, magically reproducing, egg-laying, live-bearing, spore-forming, budding, or otherwise reproducing. Where a species has sexes, the Codex can distinguish biological reproductive roles without erotic detail.

For each species, record:

- Reproductive mode
- Typical reproductive maturity
- Fertility cycle or seasonal pattern
- Gestation, incubation, or equivalent duration
- Number of offspring normally produced
- Birth/emergence characteristics
- Parental investment
- Genetic inheritance rules
- Hybridization compatibility
- Magical effects on reproduction
- Cultural attitudes toward family and lineage

## Orientation and relationships

Sexual orientation can be represented as a normal demographic and cultural trait rather than an anatomical one. Species profiles may note whether heterosexual, homosexual, bisexual, pansexual, asexual, aromantic, or other identities are recognized in the culture, and whether terminology differs by language. Individual characters should not be assigned an orientation unless established by the story.

## Comparative anatomy matrix

Every major intelligent species should eventually receive a comparison against the others. The comparison should cover the full non-graphic body from head to foot, including sensory organs, limbs, joints, integument, tails, wings, internal systems, magical anatomy, and reproductive biology at a clinical level.

The goal is to answer worldbuilding questions such as:

- Which races have homologous limbs?
- Which species have fundamentally different skeletal structures?
- Which creatures share similar eyes or sensory systems?
- Which races have tails, horns, wings, scales, fur, or specialized organs?
- Which species can interbreed, and why or why not?
- Which magical systems rely on comparable internal conduits?
- Which injuries or diseases affect multiple species similarly?
- Which anatomical assumptions cause misunderstandings between cultures?

## D&D compatibility

Where Aethelgard uses a recognizable D&D race or creature, the Codex should retain established D&D identity while adding Aethelgard-specific culture and biology. The Codex should not falsely claim that invented Aethelgard traits are official D&D canon.

## Translator separation

Biology entries are separate from language dictionaries. Anatomical terms that appear in dialogue must receive canonical translations inside the appropriate language file rather than being improvised in prose.
