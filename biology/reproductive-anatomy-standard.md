# Reproductive Biology & Comparative Anatomy Standard

## Purpose

This document defines how reproductive anatomy is recorded in the Aethelgard Codex for adult species. The purpose is educational worldbuilding: anatomy, function, development, health, and comparative biology should be clear enough for continuity checks without turning the Codex into erotic material.

## Required entry structure

Every sapient species entry should eventually include:

1. External reproductive anatomy — clinical physical description, including location, general form, protective structures, coloration/texture where biologically relevant, and visible differences between sexes or reproductive roles.
2. Internal reproductive anatomy — major organs and their functions.
3. Reproductive system type — mammalian, avian, reptilian, amphibious, insectoid, magical, or species-specific configuration.
4. Gamete production — type and biological role.
5. Fertilization — internal, external, magical, or other established mechanism.
6. Gestation/incubation — duration, environment, and developmental stages.
7. Birth/laying — offspring delivery or egg-laying process.
8. Juvenile development — major stages without sexualized framing.
9. Puberty/maturation — biological milestones and age ranges appropriate to the species' lifespan.
10. Fertility — known limits, cycles, seasonal factors, and magical influences.
11. Reproductive health — common conditions, injuries, infections, congenital differences, and cultural medical practices.
12. Cross-species compatibility — whether viable offspring are possible, uncertain, or impossible, based on Codex biology.
13. Anatomical analogues — comparable structures in other Aethelgard species.
14. Cultural interpretation — how the species understands reproduction, family, parenthood, and kinship.

## Comparative anatomy requirement

When describing an organ or external structure, the entry should identify its closest functional analogue in other species where useful. Similarity does not imply identical anatomy. For example, two species may possess a structurally different organ serving the same reproductive function.

## Clinical description standard

Descriptions should use neutral anatomical language. The entry should teach the reader what the structure is, where it is located, what tissues protect it, and what function it performs. Fantasy traits may be incorporated through scales, fur, feathers, magical membranes, external plates, specialized glands, or other established species traits.

## Aethelgard rule

No species should be treated as a human with cosmetic alterations unless the lore explicitly establishes that similarity. Differences should influence medicine, clothing, combat injuries, childbirth, magical practice, and everyday life where appropriate.

## Continuity checks

Before adding a reproductive-biology fact to a chapter, verify:

- species identity
- sex/reproductive role when relevant
- age/maturity
- anatomy already established
- magical physiology
- ancestry/hybrid status
- injuries
- medical terminology
- cultural norms
- whether the detail is actually necessary to the scene

## Privacy and narrative restraint

The Codex is a reference encyclopedia. Novel scenes should only mention reproductive anatomy when the story requires it for medicine, childbirth, biology, character identity, lore, or another legitimate narrative purpose.
