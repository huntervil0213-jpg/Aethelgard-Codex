# 19 — FISSURE CREATURES

## The name used in the novel

### Vhoruun

The Vhoruun are the creatures expelled from the violet sky-fissures. They are not elves, not Skull-Veil, and not an extension of the Skull-Veil faction. The name is deliberately separate because the Skull-Veil is the name used for the feared Elven faction.

The first witnesses in the outer townships do not have a scholarly name for them. They describe them with whatever words fear gives them: black things, bone-crowned horrors, sky-devils, ash mouths. The established Codex name is Vhoruun.

## Appearance

A Vhoruun is a massive, predatory creature composed of shifting obsidian-black material resembling living ash fused with glass. Its silhouette never remains perfectly stable. Plates, tendons, splinters and ribbons of darkness slide across one another as though the creature is continually rebuilding its own body.

Its skull is elongated and almost completely featureless. There are no visible eyes, nostrils or ears. A crown of irregular bone-like spines erupts from the rear and upper portion of the skull. The spines are not symmetrical. Some curve backward, some jut sideways, and some appear to have grown through the creature's own black tissue.

Its mouth is the dominant feature. The jaws split far wider than a humanoid anatomy should permit and contain multiple overlapping rows of serrated crimson teeth. The teeth vibrate when the creature vocalizes. Its shriek is partly physical sound and partly magical pressure; witnesses describe the sensation as hearing metal tear inside their own skulls.

The Vhoruun's limbs are disproportionately powerful. Their movement is deceptive. Despite the creature's enormous mass, it can cross short distances with sudden, almost impossible acceleration. It does not lumber. It hunts.

## Behavior

Vhoruun are predators first. They do not negotiate, reason with victims, or recognize surrender as a meaningful concept.

They are attracted to unstable magical activity, concentrated life energy and strong Flux disturbances. Settlements near reality fractures are therefore especially vulnerable.

A Vhoruun may remain completely still for several seconds before attacking. This stillness should never be interpreted as hesitation. It is usually observation.

They are capable of tracking movement through darkness and can apparently sense magical currents that ordinary creatures cannot perceive.

## The scream

The Vhoruun's primary vocalization is called the Rift-Scream by frightened survivors. It can produce terror, disorientation and temporary loss of coordination in nearby creatures. At close range, the vibration can damage hearing and concentration.

The sound should remain rare in the manuscript. Its effectiveness comes from anticipation: when characters hear the first metallic vibration in the dark, they should understand that something is close before they see it.

## Combat profile

D&D-style reference fit:

- Creature type: Aberration
- Size: Large or Huge depending on caste
- Role: Ambush predator / magical hunter
- Primary abilities: extraordinary speed, brutal bite, magical-sense tracking, fear-inducing scream, resistance to ordinary physical disruption
- Weakness direction: unstable resonance, coordinated elemental magic, and disruption of the creature's connection to the fissure
- Intelligence: predatory; higher castes may demonstrate tactical intelligence

The Vhoruun are not meant to be ordinary encounter fodder. Early appearances should establish them as creatures that make experienced fighters reconsider whether fighting is survivable.

## Castes

### Vhoruun Ravager

The common battlefield predator. It is the form first seen attacking frontier settlements.

### Vhoruun Stalker

Smaller and faster. Stalkers specialize in following magical signatures and separating fleeing victims from groups.

### Vhoruun Mawborn

An enlarged form with an exceptionally powerful Rift-Scream. Mawborn are associated with major fissures and mass attacks.

### Vhoruun Harrower

A rare form capable of interfering with magical concentration. Harrowers are especially dangerous to spellcasters and artificers.

### Vhoruun Sovereign

A theoretical apex caste. No confirmed survivor has provided a complete description. Treat its existence as development material until introduced in the manuscript.

## Relationship to the Skull-Veil

There is no factional relationship established between the Vhoruun and the Skull-Veil.

The distinction is important:

- Skull-Veil = feared Elven faction / nickname.
- Vhoruun = extradimensional creatures emerging from reality fissures.
- Vhoruun are not Skull-Veil soldiers.
- Skull-Veil elves do not control Vhoruun unless a future manuscript event explicitly establishes that connection.

## Story use

The Vhoruun should remain frightening after their introduction. They should not become friendly, comic, domesticated, or harmless merely because the protagonists understand them better.

Their greatest narrative function is to make the violet fissures feel like an active threat rather than scenery. The fissure is not simply damage in the sky. It is a doorway through which something hungry is entering Aethelgard.

## First-witness language

Outer-township survivors may use ordinary Common descriptions because they do not know the scholarly name. Examples include:

- black maw
- bone-crowned thing
- ash devil
- sky-born horror
- rift beast
- the thing from the tear

These are descriptions, not alternate species names.

## Canon status

The name Vhoruun is the current Codex canon name for the fissure creatures. Their full origin, reproduction, lifecycle, intelligence hierarchy, home reality and ultimate purpose remain open for later development.
