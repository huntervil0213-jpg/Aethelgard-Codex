# 26 — CULTURAL TERMS AND HISTORY

## Spark

Spark is an old name for humanity. The name reaches back to the belief that human life carries a peculiar capacity to kindle change wherever it settles. Humans were not the first people to walk Aethelgard, nor were they the oldest civilization. They became known as Sparks because they could take a dying settlement, an unfamiliar craft, a foreign custom, or a half-forgotten idea and make it burn again.

Among older peoples, the word has never had one fixed feeling. An elf may call a human Spark with fondness. A dwarf may use it after watching a human improvise something ridiculous and brilliant. An ancient enemy may spit the word as an accusation, remembering wars that began with a single ambitious human court.

The word therefore belongs to history as much as it belongs to race.

## Elven Terms

### Skull-Veil

Skull-Veil is a feared name attached to the elven Inquisitors known for their brutality and pursuit of those marked as threats to elven order. The name is not a synonym for elf.

In ordinary conversation, people may lower their voices when the name is spoken. In lands touched by their hunts, the name can be enough to change the mood of a room.

### Canopy House

An old elven political and scholarly institution centered on hereditary stewardship, archives, territory, and living architecture. The destruction of a canopy house can erase generations of recorded knowledge.

### High-Court Speech

The formal register used in sovereign courts, treaties, legal declarations, and diplomatic encounters. Knowing the words is not the same as knowing the etiquette behind them.

## Dwarven Terms

### Spark

Dwarves sometimes use Spark for humans in a teasingly respectful sense, particularly when a human's reckless creativity reminds them of a forge catching unexpectedly.

### Forge-Born

An old dwarven honorific for someone whose identity is deeply tied to craft, metal, stone, or engineering.

### Iron-Marrow

A traditional dwarven expression for stubborn endurance. It can be an honorific when spoken seriously and an insult when suggesting someone is too stubborn to change.

## Feline-Kin Terms

### Softpaw

A teasing term for someone inexperienced at surviving outside protected communities. Among friends it can be affectionate; from a stranger it can be patronizing.

### Nine-Luck

An old expression for improbable survival. It refers to the cultural belief that fortune sometimes follows those who should have died long ago.

## Human Terms

### Spark

The most widespread old poetic name for humanity.

### High-Reach

The political and architectural center associated with the ruling powers of the Spire civilization.

### Blood Right

The inherited political status claimed by certain royal houses. Losing blood right is more than losing a title; it can mean being severed from the legal identity of an entire dynasty.

## Frontier Terms

### Silt-Sinks

A degraded industrial region shaped by extraction, pollution, and the collapse of older environmental systems.

### Iron-Vein

The name given to extensive filtration and extraction networks built around mineral-rich underground systems. The term can refer to the physical network or the industrial system surrounding it.

### Flux-Damned

A curse used in regions where unstable magic has become part of everyday life. Depending on tone, it can express anger, disbelief, or genuine fear.

### Void Take You

A harsher curse invoking the unknown forces beyond ordinary reality. It is particularly fitting when a character is frightened by a reality fissure or supernatural threat.

### By Solis

A common religious exclamation associated with Solis. Characters can use it casually, reverently, sarcastically, or desperately depending on the situation.

## Social Meaning

Aethelgard's vocabulary carries memory. Words survive because something happened that made people need them. A curse can preserve an old fear. A nickname can preserve an old war. A friendly expression can preserve a cultural exchange that happened thousands of years earlier.

The language of a people should therefore reveal where they came from, what they remember, and what they have learned to fear.
