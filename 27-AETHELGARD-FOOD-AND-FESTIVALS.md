# 27 — FOOD, FESTIVALS, AND SMALL CUSTOMS

## The Hearthwake

A winter gathering observed in several human settlements. Families place a small flame in the center of the table and keep it burning through the evening. The fire represents the first spark carried into a cold world.

## Greenwake

An elven spring observance marking the return of active growth. Canopy communities hang living vines across doorways rather than using cloth decorations.

## Deepforge Night

A dwarven celebration held after major communal construction. The finished work is walked around in silence before the first meal is served.

## Nine-Luck Supper

A Feline-Kin meal traditionally served with nine small dishes. Each dish represents a different kind of fortune: safe travel, good health, cleverness, friendship, patience, courage, memory, prosperity, and return.

## Common Foods

### Sweet-root

A luminous root cultivated in fertile magical soils. Its flesh is naturally sweet and is commonly roasted, candied, or folded into bread.

### Mossbread

A frontier bread made with dried edible moss, grain, salt, and root flour. Different regions argue over the proper ratio.

### Embercap Mushrooms

Red-capped cave mushrooms that develop a faint warmth when cooked. Dwarven cooks use them in stews and forge-house meals.

### Starfruit

A pale fruit whose skin reflects light in tiny points. It grows in several ley-rich regions.

### Rivergrain

A hardy grain cultivated beside clean waterways. It forms the basis of many simple travel meals.

## Old Table Customs

Leaving the final portion of a meal for a guest is considered courteous in many cultures.

Dwarven households may tap a cup against the table before drinking to remember those who worked beneath the same roof.

Some elven families eat the first meal of spring outdoors so the living roots can hear the family's voices.

Human travelers sometimes place a tiny pinch of salt beside their bread before leaving home. The practice is associated with the belief that a journey should begin with something that belongs to the hearth.

## Markets

Markets across Aethelgard reveal cultural differences more clearly than royal proclamations. Spices, preserved roots, enchanted fruits, smoked meats, fermented grains, crystal candies, medicinal herbs, and imported tea can all indicate where communities have traded over the centuries.

## Children's Games

### Sky Stones

Children toss polished stones upward and attempt to catch them in sequence. The game originated among travelers and later spread widely.

### Root Runner

An elven game played around living roots. Players must cross a marked grove without touching the same root twice.

### Forgehand

A dwarven guessing game in which players identify metals by weight, sound, and temperature without looking.

### Spark Chase

A human children's game involving lanterns. One child carries the Spark while the others attempt to catch them before the light reaches a designated hearth.

## Festival Memory

Festivals often preserve history long after formal records disappear. A dance may commemorate an ancient migration. A particular food may originate from a famine. A children's game may contain the movements of an old military drill.

Aethelgard's cultures therefore carry history not only in books and monuments, but in kitchens, games, songs, and ordinary evenings.
