# 28 — OLD FAITHS AND RITUALS

## Solis

Solis is associated with light, endurance, guidance, and the persistence of life through darkness. Worship varies from formal temples to small household traditions.

## The First Flame

An old belief holds that the first mortal fire was not merely a tool. It represented the moment life learned to answer darkness with its own light. Human Spark traditions preserve this idea strongly.

## Root Memory

Several elven traditions teach that living forests remember events through their roots. This belief influences burial customs, architecture, and ancestral ceremonies.

## The Stone Witness

Dwarven funerary tradition sometimes places a worked stone beside the dead. The stone is shaped by the deceased's family and remains unfinished on one side, symbolizing work that no life completely completes.

## The Returning Path

Feline-Kin travelers may perform a small ritual when returning home: touching the threshold, turning once toward the road, and speaking the names of those who waited for them.

## Oaths

Ancient oaths vary dramatically between cultures. Some invoke gods. Others invoke ancestors, rivers, mountains, stars, or the living land itself.

## Burial Traditions

Elven communities may return bodies to living groves.

Dwarven clans often place remains in protected ancestral halls or deep stone chambers.

Human traditions differ by region, religion, and family.

Dragonborn communities may preserve scales, weapons, or written names as ancestral objects.

## Pilgrimage

The oldest pilgrimages were not always religious. Some were journeys to ancestral settlements, ancient battlefields, first-forge sites, or places where a civilization believed its people first awakened to magic.

## Heresy

Heresy is rarely a single belief across Aethelgard. One kingdom may condemn a practice another considers sacred. Ancient conflicts over divine authority have left entire regions with incompatible religious histories.

## Shrines

A shrine can be as elaborate as a temple or as simple as a stone with a candle beside it. Travelers frequently create temporary shrines when crossing dangerous country.

## The Unnamed Dead

One old custom says that when a person dies without anyone knowing their name, the community should give them a temporary name before burial. The practice is meant to ensure that nobody disappears from memory without even a word to mark their existence.
