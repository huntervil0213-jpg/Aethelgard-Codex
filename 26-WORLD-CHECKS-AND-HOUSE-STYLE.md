# 26 — WORLD CHECKS & HOUSE STYLE

This is the working checklist I use when adding to or editing the Aethelgard material.

## Story first

The novel comes first. Codex material exists to support the story, not to force the story to obey a database entry that was invented later.

## Character check

Before changing or adding anything, check:

- name
- age if established
- race
- appearance
- class or combat role
- known abilities
- injuries
- equipment
- relationships
- speech pattern
- emotional state
- established history
- secrets and reveals

## World check

Check:

- location
- time of day
- weather
- travel distance
- who is physically present
- political situation
- local customs
- magical conditions
- technology level
- racial terminology
- known historical events

## Language check

Fictional languages need:

- stable spelling
- stable meaning
- distinct vocabulary
- cultural context
- different registers
- believable irregularity
- pronunciation guidance where useful
- translator-friendly romanization

Do not make every invented word resemble another invented word.

## D&D check

Use recognizable D&D concepts naturally when they fit:

- classes
- subclasses
- races/species
- monsters
- spells
- conditions
- armor
- weapons
- magic items
- planes
- deities
- adventuring terminology

Established Aethelgard terms take priority when the novel has already defined something differently.

## Dialogue check

Characters should not all sound like the same writer. Dialogue should reflect education, culture, class, profession, personality, stress, and relationships.

Thrum can sound like Thrum.
Elara can sound like Elara.
Kaelen can sound like Kaelen.
Abel can sound like Abel.
Lyra can sound like Lyra.
Vaelin can sound like Vaelin.
Kiki can sound like Kiki.

## Human prose check

Avoid:

- repetitive sentence formulas
- excessive dramatic fragments
- constant em-dash interruptions
- thesaurus-heavy vocabulary
- artificial exposition
- characters explaining information they already know
- every scene ending with a dramatic hook
- repeated words when a natural alternative exists
- identical paragraph lengths
- suspiciously symmetrical dialogue

The prose should feel written, revised, and lived-in rather than mechanically generated.

## Emotional check

Fear, guilt, anger, affection, grief, attraction, shame, and relief should have physical and behavioral consequences.

Characters should not instantly recover because the scene needs to move forward.

## Prejudice check

Racist language belongs to characters and cultures when the story calls for it, not to the narrator's worldview. Slurs should have history and purpose rather than appearing as random edgy vocabulary.

## Canon status

Each Codex entry should be treated as one of three states:

**Established:** directly present in the novel or explicitly confirmed by the author.

**Working canon:** deliberately created for the Codex and intended for future story use.

**Open:** an idea that exists only as a possibility and should not be treated as established fact.

## Final pass

Before a section is considered finished, check continuity, terminology, language, character voice, D&D compatibility, emotional logic, pacing, repetition, and whether anything new accidentally contradicts the manuscript.

The point of the Codex is to make writing the novel easier, not to make the novel read like the Codex.
