# Aethelgard Language Design Specification

## Purpose

Every constructed language in the Codex must function as a repeatable language, not a collection of fantasy-looking fragments.

## Required data for each language

- Canonical name
- Native speakers
- Geographic range
- Historical origin
- Related languages
- Dialects
- Formal register
- Casual register
- Military register
- Religious/ritual register
- Insults and profanity
- Greetings and farewells
- Titles and honorifics
- Pronouns
- Demonstratives
- Question words
- Numbers
- Time vocabulary
- Kinship vocabulary
- Nature vocabulary
- Government vocabulary
- Military vocabulary
- Magic vocabulary
- Technology vocabulary
- Body/anatomical vocabulary
- Emotional vocabulary
- Common idioms
- Grammar rules
- Word-order rules

- Transliteration rules
- Canonical dictionary entries

## Aethelgard constructed-language rule

A newly invented language must be audibly distinct from Common/English and from the other constructed languages. Avoid simply respelling English words. Roots should be internally reusable and should generate related vocabulary through documented morphology.

## Translator requirement

Every canonical entry must have a stable transliterated form suitable for an external text translator. The database form should use predictable ASCII spelling unless a separate native-script field is supplied. Native scripts may be recorded independently.

## Chapter editing rule

When editing dialogue in a constructed language:

1. Prefer existing canonical vocabulary.
2. Use grammar from the language specification.
3. Vary sentence structure naturally.
4. Do not repeat the same root in every sentence merely to make dialogue look foreign.
5. Let context establish meaning when a literal translation would interrupt the scene.
6. Preserve translations already established in the manuscript.
7. If a speaker is rusty, reflect that through hesitation, self-correction, archaic vocabulary, or searching for a word rather than making the language grammatically random.
8. Keep each culture's language recognizable by sound and structure.

## Lyra's High Elven constraint

Lyra learned High Elven through archival study and diplomatic texts rather than ordinary conversation. Her command may therefore be grammatically educated but rusty in spontaneous speech. She can understand formal constructions better than she can produce casual or rapidly spoken dialogue. Her speech should occasionally show hesitation or retrieval difficulty without making her incompetent.

## Translation layers

Each language should eventually support:

- Native script
- Scholarly transliteration
- Literal gloss
- Natural Common translation
- Register
- Speaker/context notes

The natural Common translation is what belongs in the novel when readers need to understand the sentence. The literal gloss is a reference tool and should not automatically appear in prose.
