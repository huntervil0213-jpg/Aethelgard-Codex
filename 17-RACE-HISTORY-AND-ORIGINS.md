# 17 — RACE HISTORY & ORIGINS

## Purpose

This is the long-form historical framework for the peoples and creatures of Aethelgard. These entries distinguish ancient ancestry from modern political identity. A race can have several origin traditions, divergent branches, magical adaptations, and regional cultures.

## 1. Dragons and Dragonborn

Dragons and Dragonborn are related, but they are not the same people and Dragonborn did not replace dragons.

In the earliest eras, true dragons were among the oldest intelligent mortal-adjacent beings to interact with the world's magical currents. Their bodies naturally carried enormous elemental and arcane potential.

During an ancient period remembered in different cultures as the **Shattering of Scale**, some dragon lineages entered into long-term magical covenants with mortal peoples. These covenants altered descendants over many generations. Some branches gradually became the distinct Dragonborn peoples known in later ages.

The transformation was not universal. Many dragons rejected the covenants entirely. Others remained isolated, and some dragon bloodlines produced several divergent mortal descendants.

Therefore modern Aethelgard can contain:

- ancient true dragons
- chromatic dragons
- metallic dragons
- gem dragons
- lesser draconic beasts
- Dragonborn descended from ancient draconic covenants
- sorcerous bloodlines carrying partial draconic inheritance

Dragonborn are not simply weakened dragons. Their cultures developed independently over enormous spans of time.

## 2. Elves

Elven ancestry reaches into the oldest periods of Aethelgard. Early elves lived in close relationship with forests, seasonal magic, spirits, and ley currents.

Over countless generations, different elven societies developed distinct traditions. High-court Elven, canopy cultures, border traditions, shadow communities, and scholarly houses can therefore differ dramatically while sharing deep ancestry.

Kael-Vossen represents one surviving branch of an older ecological civilization. Its architecture reflects a philosophy in which living systems and constructed systems cooperate rather than compete.

## 3. Dwarves

Dwarven civilization developed around stone, deep-earth resources, metallurgy, engineering, and eventually magical machinery. Ancient dwarven cultures were among the first to discover repeatable methods for storing force inside crafted materials.

Not every dwarf became an engineer. Warrior clans, miners, rune-smiths, merchants, priests, explorers, and archivists developed alongside technological traditions.

Modern dwarves therefore retain both deep ancestral craft traditions and newer industrial techniques.

## 4. Feline-Kin

Feline-Kin descend from several ancient populations shaped by forest, desert, and urban environments. Their ancestors were highly mobile hunter-gatherers before developing sophisticated trade networks.

Different Feline-Kin communities developed radically different attitudes toward scholarship, commerce, stealth, spirituality, and family structure.

Kiki's people should not be treated as a single monolithic culture.

## 5. Humans

Humans appeared comparatively late in the oldest surviving historical records, but their adaptability allowed them to establish enormous civilizations quickly.

Human populations absorbed customs, technologies, languages, and magical practices from older peoples. This helped produce extraordinary diversity but also enabled ambitious kingdoms to appropriate knowledge from other civilizations.

House Valerius is one historical expression of that tendency, not a definition of humanity as a whole.

## 6. Halflings

Halfling populations emerged along river plains and fertile lowlands. Their early cultures were strongly tied to agriculture, trade, oral history, and community-based survival.

Many ancient halfling settlements became important neutral trading grounds during the True Alliance.

The displacement of halfling populations during imperial expansion is one of the historical wounds referenced by Queen Althaea.

## 7. Tieflings

Tiefling ancestry is associated with ancient planar contact, infernal pacts, magical mutation, and several older bloodlines. No single origin explains every Tiefling family.

Some lineages descend from deliberate pacts. Others emerged through generations of planar influence. Still others developed traits after exposure to unstable magical regions.

This diversity is important: Tieflings are a people, not a single supernatural experiment.

## 8. Giants

Giants predate many mortal kingdoms. Ancient giant cultures organized themselves around elemental and environmental domains: storm, stone, fire, frost, cloud, and other regional powers.

Some giant bloodlines diminished as mortal civilizations expanded. Others withdrew into remote regions rather than disappear.

## 9. Gnomes

Gnomish civilizations developed around experimentation, illusion, clockwork, alchemy, and magical curiosity. Their oldest ruins suggest that some gnomish scholars understood principles of artificial magical focusing long before the modern Spires.

## 10. Orcs

Orc history is much older than the common imperial stereotype of raiders and mercenaries. Ancient orc societies developed warrior traditions, agricultural settlements, spiritual institutions, clan law, and sophisticated oral genealogies.

Many imperial records intentionally reduced these cultures to military threats.

## 11. Goblins, Hobgoblins, and Bugbears

These related peoples diverged culturally over extremely long periods. Goblin communities often specialized in scavenging, adaptation, trade, and improvised technology. Hobgoblin societies developed disciplined military and civic structures. Bugbear groups became associated with hunting, ambush, and wilderness survival.

Their shared ancestry does not erase their distinct cultures.

## 12. Aasimar

Aasimar bloodlines developed through ancient celestial influence. Some families experienced direct divine contact; others inherited diluted traits over many generations.

Their appearance and abilities vary considerably between lineages.

## 13. Genasi

Genasi emerged through long-term elemental influence upon mortal bloodlines. Air, earth, fire, and water traditions are the most widely recognized, but rare regional expressions can exist.

## 14. Gith

The Gith descend from an ancient people whose history became entangled with other planes. Their separation into major cultural branches occurred after prolonged enslavement and rebellion.

Their oldest records preserve memories that many material-plane civilizations consider myth.

## 15. Aarakocra

Aarakocra cultures developed around high-altitude environments and strong relationships with wind and sky magic. Their oldest settlements were difficult for ground-bound empires to reach, allowing many traditions to survive independently.

## 16. Kenku

Kenku communities developed distinctive traditions surrounding memory, imitation, craft, and oral preservation. Their histories demonstrate that a culture can preserve enormous amounts of information without conventional written archives.

## 17. Kobolds

Kobold ancestry is frequently connected to ancient draconic influence, but modern kobold cultures are independent peoples with their own societies, crafts, religions, and languages.

## 18. Tortles

Tortle societies developed along warm coasts and wetlands. Their traditions emphasize travel, patience, oral memory, and environmental awareness.

## 19. Tabaxi

Tabaxi cultures share ancient feline ancestry with other feline peoples in some regional traditions, while other histories describe independent magical origins. The exact relationship varies by continent and era.

## 20. Yuan-ti

Yuan-ti histories trace back to ancient civilizations that deliberately altered their bodies through serpent magic. Their transformation occurred through ritual, selective bloodlines, and magical experimentation rather than a single event.

## 21. Lizardfolk

Lizardfolk cultures are among the oldest surviving swamp and marsh societies. Their survival strategies emphasize environmental adaptation and practical communal knowledge.

## 22. Firbolgs

Firbolg traditions preserve ancient relationships with forests, giants, and fey magic. Many communities deliberately withdrew from expanding civilizations.

## 23. Shifters

Shifter ancestry reflects ancient lycanthropic and beast-touched bloodlines. Their societies developed ways to manage inherited transformations without allowing them to define every aspect of identity.

## 24. Warforged and Artificial Peoples

Artificial humanoid life emerged much later than most biological peoples. Early constructs were tools. Later magical engineering produced beings capable of independent thought.

The existence of such peoples forced philosophers to reconsider whether a soul must originate biologically.

## 25. Fey, Elementals, and Other Ancient Beings

Not every intelligent species follows the same evolutionary history. Fey creatures, elementals, celestials, fiends, undead, aberrations, and certain primordial creatures may originate from planes, magical forces, divine creation, or realities outside ordinary mortal ancestry.

Their histories should therefore be recorded separately from mortal racial genealogies.

## 26. The Important Rule

A race's present-day appearance does not reveal its complete history. A modern people may be the result of migration, magical adaptation, intermarriage, planar influence, divine intervention, ancient pacts, or deliberate transformation across thousands or millions of years.
