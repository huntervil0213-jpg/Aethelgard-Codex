# LANGUAGES

This is where I keep track of the languages used in Aethelgard. The main focus is making the languages feel like real languages instead of a pile of fantasy-sounding words.

## ELVEN

The elven languages have several forms depending on region, age, and social class. The court form is especially formal and is used for law, diplomacy, royal commands, and official declarations.

### Kael-Vossen Court Speech

This is the formal elven variety used by the ruling houses and border authorities around Kael-Vossen.

It needs its own vocabulary, grammar, sound patterns, titles, greetings, military terms, insults, and everyday expressions. Words should not simply be Common words with an elven-looking ending.

Lyra learned older forms of the language from her family's archives. She understands much more than she can comfortably speak. She can recognize old grammar and formal expressions, but she is rusty when actually speaking it because she never had regular conversation in the language.

### Translation Rules

- Keep the constructed language separate from Common.
- Avoid words that obviously sound like English or Common.
- Do not reuse the same invented words for unrelated meanings.
- Keep established vocabulary consistent once a meaning is assigned.
- Formal speech should sound different from ordinary conversation.
- Military commands should be short and recognizable.
- Older court speech can differ from modern forms.
- Lyra may hesitate, search for words, correct grammar, or use an older construction.
- Meaning should normally be understandable from context, not explained after every sentence.

## IMPORTANT

I want the languages in this world to be usable when writing dialogue. I should be able to look up a word, see what it means, know who would use it, and understand how it fits into a sentence.

The Aethelgard language system should also stay compatible with the D&D language framework where possible, while still having its own vocabulary and writing systems.
